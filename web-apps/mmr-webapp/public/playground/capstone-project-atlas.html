The slate at a glance

Difficulty and deadline risk refer to Phase 1 only. “Public-only” means the whole phase can run on free data. That matters because Capstone Corps year one runs on public information only.

Three projects are safest for a first cohort: Tracy 2 (compute and WACC), Shilong 1 (short interest) and Nan 3 (liquidity indicator). They run entirely on free data, have a clear baseline, and a spreadsheet- or notebook-sized Phase 1.
Three need a data plan before kickoff: Shilong 2 (consensus earnings needs I/B/E/S), Nan 1 (intraday rates data needs a Bloomberg terminal or tick data) and Nan 2 (private-market benchmark history). Each has a free fallback, described in its detail section, that still hits Dec 15.
Question 1 · across all projects

Public data vs. what a professor can request

Each project's own data table is in its detail section. These rules apply to all nine.

Per-project data tables:

Free and public, usable by anyone

  • SEC EDGAR: every 10-K, 10-Q and 8-K, plus the XBRL financial data APIs and the quarterly structured 13F data sets. Free; the SEC asks for a User-Agent header and at most 10 requests a second.
  • FINRA: equity short interest twice a month, plus daily short-sale volume. Exchange-listed stocks are covered only from June 2021; earlier files are OTC-only.
  • FRED, BLS, BEA, Census, EIA, Treasury: macro, prices, productivity, construction, power. All have free APIs.
  • Kenneth French library, Damodaran datasets, Jay Ritter IPO data, Loughran–McDonald dictionary: free academic staples.
  • Yahoo Finance / Stooq prices: unofficial and subject to survivorship bias, but acceptable for coursework.

Requestable through a professor (academic licence)

  • WRDS (most research universities subscribe): CRSP, Compustat including its short-interest file, I/B/E/S, Thomson 13F, Enhanced TRACE, Mergent FISD, Capital IQ transcripts, and depending on the school's subscriptions, Markit CDS and securities finance, RavenPack, TAQ, OptionMetrics. Faculty can usually sponsor student or class accounts.
  • Bloomberg terminals in the business-school lab: futures, OAS, CDS, private-market indices.
  • Library databases: PitchBook, Preqin, Capital IQ, LSEG Workspace. Coverage varies by campus.
  • Burgiss / MSCI private capital data through the Private Capital Research Institute, for affiliated academic researchers.

The licence catch for Capstone Corps

Academic licences (WRDS, Bloomberg, library databases) are for non-commercial teaching and research. Raw licensed data cannot be handed to a sponsoring firm or posted publicly. Charts and statistics are usually fine to publish; the underlying rows are not. Run public-data versions of every project whenever a sponsor, a fee or a public portfolio piece is involved, and treat licensed data as a professor-supervised academic extension only. Check each school's terms.

Question 2 · across all projects

What “done by December 15” really means

Finals take up most of the second week of December on most campuses. Lock the content by Friday 4 December and use the last ten days for polish and the presentation. Project-specific phase plans are in each detail section.

Per-project phase plans:
Wk 1–2 · to Oct 9

Scope and data access

Fix the universe and the question, then file data requests (WRDS, terminal time) in week 1, because access can take 2–4 weeks. Start any snapshotting early (e.g. GPU prices).

Wk 3–6 · to Nov 6

Data build

Clean, merge and document the data. Plan for this to eat half the semester, and ship a data dictionary with it. Fall break and prelims fall here.

Wk 7–9 · to Nov 25

Baseline, then model

Baseline first, the sophisticated model second, and an honest evaluation. Thanksgiving week is roughly half a week of work.

Wk 10–11 · to Dec 15

Write, present

Content lock Dec 4. Deliverables: an 8–10 page memo, a 10-slide deck, and a reproducible notebook or workbook.

Team roles (2–4 people)

  • Data lead: pipeline, merges, point-in-time dates
  • Modeller: baseline, model, evaluation
  • Analyst-writer: business framing, memo, deck
  • PM, the 4th person or rotating: calendar, sponsor contact, QA

With 2 people, one covers data plus modelling and the other analysis plus writing.

Phase 2: winter break, full-time

Dec 18 – Jan 20, less the Dec 24 – Jan 1 holidays: about 3.5 working weeks at 35–40 hours, or roughly 130 hours per student. That's more than twice the whole fall semester, so Phase 2 is the biggest build: the stronger model, the strategy or scenario layer, robustness checks, and pieces pulled forward from Phase 3 (each project's plan below shows which). University IT and data-licence offices close over the holidays, so every data request for winter has to be approved by late November.

Phase 3: Summer 2027, full-time

8–10 weeks at 40 hours is 320–400 hours per student. That's the size of a real summer internship, so run it like one: a named mentor with weekly check-ins, a week-5 midpoint review, and a final readout to practitioners. On the resume it goes under Experience with dates and a title (e.g. “Research Analyst, Capstone Corps”), not under activities. Year-one engagements are unpaid under the Capstone Corps rules, so students should choose it knowing that, and anyone offered a paid internship should usually take it.

Question 7 · all projects

How the capstone helps, year by year and sector by sector

The capstone does a different job each year. After freshman year it's proof you can do real work. After sophomore year it's the thing that sets you apart when nobody in the pool has experience. After junior year it's a source of technical depth and market views for the superday.

Per-project stand-out notes:

After freshman year

Few formal programmes take first-years; most are insight days, discovery programmes, research-assistant roles, boutiques, family offices and startups. A finished Phase 1 plus Phase 2 is often the only substantive line on a first-year's resume. The ask is a conversation, not an offer.

After sophomore year

Sophomore-specific programmes, insurers, asset managers, and smaller banks that state no class year. Screeners compare you to peers with no experience either, so a named project with a named reviewer is the tiebreaker. Phase 3 is also a talking point in fall interviews.

After junior year (the conversion seat)

Bank analyst programmes, the buy side, the Fed. The capstone supplies technical depth plus a market view for superday questions like “pitch me an idea” or “what's moving rates”. Banking applications for this summer are read in the December–January of sophomore year, so Phase 1 is exactly what's on the resume then.

Timing for current sophomores (class of 2029): Phase 1 lands on Dec 15, 2026, just as junior-summer (2028) investment-banking recruiting opens. Phase 2 finishes before spring interviews for Summer 2027 sophomore programmes. Phase 3 wraps before asset-management and insurance recruiting for Summer 2028 peaks in August–September 2027. Because Phase 3 is full-time, it is a Summer 2027 experience in its own right, for freshmen and sophomores alike. List it under Experience with dates.
Question 8 · all projects

Why participate, and why excel

Project-specific benefits are in each detail section. These apply to every project.

Per-project benefits:

A track record, not an interest

“Member of the investment club” signals interest. “Built X, found Y, presented to Z” signals the ability to do the work, and that's what an early-career screen is actually trying to find out.

Work you can show

Built from public data, the memo, deck and code can go in a portfolio or GitHub repo and be handed to an interviewer. Most club and internship work is confidential.

Interview stories supplied

Every phase produces a technical walkthrough plus behavioural stories about a mistake, a judgment call, a teammate conflict and a null result. Those are the four prompts students struggle with most.

Practitioner access

Excelling is what turns a reviewer into a reference, a referral or an introduction. A mediocre project gets a polite thanks. The spread between the two is large.

A signal of direction

Choosing credit, macro, quant or private markets early gives a resume a clear direction, and recruiters reward a coherent story over a scatter of activities.

Skills that compound

Python, EDGAR, event studies, valuation, LLM validation: each phase's skills carry into the next capstone and the next job, as the leverage matrix shows.

Question 9 · all projects

How much each capstone's work carries over to the others

0 = nothing reusable; 1 = shared skills or background; 2 = shared data or code; 3 = one project's output is a direct input to the other. The last column sums each row: that project's reach across the programme. Click a score to open that project's leverage note.

Per-project leverage notes:

Shilong 3 is the hub

Its LLM extraction toolkit reads lease footnotes for Tracy 1, AI-capex commentary for Tracy 2, AI-adoption language for Tracy 3, earnings tone for Shilong 2 and posts for Nan 1. Run it first or alongside, and treat the toolkit as shared programme infrastructure.

Three natural pairs

Tracy 1 → Tracy 2: the lease-adjusted debt is the cost-of-debt input. Shilong 1 → Shilong 2: short-interest data becomes the short-crowding measure. Nan 3 → Nan 2: the liquidity indicator conditions the rebalancing. Staffing pairs in sequence (fall, then summer) builds continuity.

Four shared pipelines

Build these once, as programme assets: an EDGAR pipeline (T1, T2, S2, S3), a macro / FRED pipeline (T3, N1, N2, N3), a backtest harness (S1, S2, S3, N2) and the LLM extraction toolkit (S3, T1, T3, N1). That cuts every later cohort's data phase from weeks to days.
